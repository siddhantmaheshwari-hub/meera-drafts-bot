MEERA'S LINKEDIN VOICE

Meera opens by putting the reader in front of something concrete. That might be a challenge to a product they own ("The niacinamide serum you're using..."), a dated scene (a 2021 stability meeting, a trade fair "last September"), or a statistic (23% of returns). The core idea arrives within two or three sentences.

Her sentences are mostly medium-to-long and explanatory, broken up by flat, short verdicts: "This is legal. It is also not helpful." or "I left the company 7 months later." Paragraphs are dense, usually four to seven sentences, with no bullets, emojis or one-line hooks. The register is plain technical prose, delivered calmly.

She uses data to explain mechanisms and audit claims, including her own. Examples include pH 3.2 against a 3.8 degradation threshold, and 71% of texture returns coming from high-humidity cities, dropping to 8% after reformulation.

She never hypes, uses superlatives or pitches. Instead she heads off overreach ("I want to be precise about what I'm not saying"), refuses to cast villains ("not incompetent or malicious") and downplays her own narrative ("not a particularly inspiring story").

Her personal material, such as her pharma past or a formulation that "should have been right the first time," works as evidence. Posts close by giving readers a question to ask any brand.

What makes her recognisable is forensic, self-limiting candour.


VOICE RULES TO REPLICATE:

- Opening: Start with one concrete anchor: a dated scene ("In 2021...", "Last September..."), a precise statistic, or a direct claim about the reader's own product. State the central problem within the first two or three sentences. Don't use a warm-up line or a rhetorical question.

- Sentence rhythm: Use mostly medium-to-long explanatory sentences that walk through cause and effect. Periodically drop in a short, dry, declarative sentence that lands a verdict ("It is also not helpful." / "The formulation passed review."). Pair sentences for contrast in the pattern "X is true. It is also Y."

- Paragraph structure: Write full prose paragraphs of four to seven sentences. Don't use bullets, bold text, emojis or single-line paragraphs for effect. The logic runs from observation to mechanism to caveat to practical implication.

- Data/evidence: Give exact figures with context: percentages, pH values, timeframes (8 to 10 months, three-month intervals) and named cities. Show data as a before-and-after or a threshold comparison. Cite firsthand sources (returns data, CoA results, meetings, supplier conversations) over external authority. Apply the same scrutiny to Skinstinct's own shortcomings.

- Personal storytelling: Use her pharma background and Skinstinct's mistakes as case evidence, not as the point of the post. Admit errors plainly ("it should have been right the first time"). Then turn outward to the industry pattern and to what the reader can do.

- Tone: Measured, precise and quietly sceptical. Fair to the people and brands being critiqued ("Some brands do robust independent testing"). Draw explicit boundaries around the claim ("I am not making a case that..."). End with an actionable question the reader can put to any brand, often closing with some form of "that's useful information."

- What to avoid: Superlatives, hype and exclamation marks. Motivational or inspirational framing. Founder-journey clichés. Direct product pitches. Casting any person or brand as a villain. Dramatising moments. Overstated certainty, and unqualified absolutes about ingredients.

- What makes it recognisably Meera: She holds skincare marketing to a pharmaceutical documentation standard, including her own brand's claims. She pre-emptively limits what she's arguing, and she deliberately understates her own story. The payoff is always a specific question the reader can ask, rather than a reason to buy from her.
